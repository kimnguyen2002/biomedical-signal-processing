I. Mathematical Symbols and Notation#

I collect the main conventions here so you can look up a symbol without losing your place in a chapter. Symbols get their meaning from the local definition: the same letter can stand for different quantities in different models.

I.1 Signals, time, and frequency#

Symbol

Read it as

Meaning and units

\(x(t)\)

x at time t

Continuous-time signal; e.g. mV

\(x[n]\)

x at sample n

A discrete-time sequence

\(t\)

Time

Seconds unless stated otherwise

\(n\)

Sample index

Integer count

\(f_s\)

Sampling frequency

Samples per second, in Hz

\(T_s\)

Sampling interval

\(1/f_s\), in seconds

\(N\)

Number of samples

A count, unless locally redefined

\(f\)

Frequency

Cycles per second, in Hz

\(\omega\)

Angular frequency

\(2\pi f\), in rad/s

\(\Omega\)

Discrete angular frequency

\(2\pi f/f_s\), in rad/sample

\(\varphi\)

Phase

Usually radians

For example, \(x[n]=x(nT_s)\) evaluates a continuous waveform at the time of sample \(n\). At \(f_s=360\) Hz, sample 180 represents time 0.5 s.

I.3 Sums and calculus#

\[ \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]=x[0]+x[1]+\cdots+x[N-1]. \]

The sum has \(N\) terms. If \(x=[1,2,3]\), it is 6; dividing by three gives a sample mean of 2.

Symbol

Meaning

\(\sum\)

Add the indexed terms

\(\prod\)

Multiply the indexed terms

\(dx/dt\)

Rate of change of \(x\) with time

\(\int_a^b x(t)\,dt\)

Accumulation between \(a\) and \(b\)

\(\lim\)

Value approached in a stated limit

\(\infty\)

Unbounded extent, not an ordinary finite number

If \(x\) is measured in mV and \(t\) in seconds, its derivative is in mV/s and its time integral is in mV·s. Units provide a useful check on a derivation.

Symbol

Meaning

\(j\)

Imaginary unit, \(j^2=-1\); Python writes 1j

\(z^*\)

Complex conjugate

\(\operatorname{Re}\{z\}\), \(\operatorname{Im}\{z\}\)

Real and imaginary parts

\(e^{j\theta}\)

\(\cos\theta+j\sin\theta\)

\(\mathbf{x}\), \(\mathbf{A}\)

Vector and matrix

\(\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf T}\)

Transpose

\(\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf H}\)

Conjugate transpose

\(\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert_2\)

For \(z=3+4j\), the conjugate is \(3-4j\) and the magnitude is \(\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5\).

I.5 Probability and signal operations#

Symbol

Meaning

\(\mathbb{E}[X]\)

Expected value, a probability-weighted average

\(\mu\)

Mean when defined that way in the model

\(\sigma^2\)

Variance

\(\hat{x}\)

Estimate of \(x\)

\(\bar{x}\)

Average of \(x\)

\(x*h\)

Convolution

\(\delta[n]\)

Discrete unit impulse

\(\delta(t)\)

Continuous Dirac impulse, a distribution

\(X(f)\)

Fourier transform under the stated convention

\(H\)

Frequency response or transfer function, depending on its argument

The star between signals denotes convolution; a superscript star denotes conjugation. Also distinguish variance \(\sigma^2\) from conductivity \(\sigma\) in a volume-conductor model. Check definitions and units each time.
